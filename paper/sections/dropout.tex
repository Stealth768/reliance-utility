\section{Case study: modality dropout in a two-view proxy}
\label{sec:fusion}

\paragraph{Setup.}
To examine whether training with modality dropout changes reliance on a
degraded input, we train small late-fusion networks on Fashion-MNIST with
each image split into a left half (view A) and a right half (view B). This
is a \emph{two-view proxy} and not a multimodal benchmark. Rather than using
the standard 10,000-image evaluation split, the data pipeline loads the 60,000-image
training split and partitions it deterministically into 50,000 training, 5,000 validation,
and 5,000 test images (indices 55,000--60,000). View A is
degraded by interpolation toward noise on a nine-point severity grid; the
no-input baseline replaces view A by a learned null embedding. The fusion
gate does not receive the severity. We train with view-A dropout
probabilities $p\in\{0,0.1,0.25,0.5\}$ and five seeds.

\paragraph{Pre-declared hypotheses.}
We declared before training: (H1) the Misalignment Index is non-increasing
in $p$; (H2) clean accuracy falls by less than three points between $p=0$
and $p=0.25$.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:fusion} reports the outcome. H2 holds. H1 does \emph{not}:
the Misalignment Index is higher for every $p>0$ than for $p=0$. Between
$p=0$ and $p=0.25$, clean accuracy changes from
\checknum{0.888}{fusion:0.0:clean_acc} to
\checknum{0.884}{fusion:0.25:clean_acc}, a decrease of only
\checknum{0.364}{fusion:diff_0_25:clean_acc_pp} percentage points,
well within the pre-declared three-point bound.
Dropout-trained models achieve much higher accuracy when view A is removed
(increasing from \checknum{0.562}{fusion:0.0:acc_null_removed} at $p=0$ to
\checknum{0.840}{fusion:0.1:acc_null_removed}--\checknum{0.858}{fusion:0.5:acc_null_removed}
for $p\ge 0.1$) and when it is fully degraded
(increasing from \checknum{0.276}{fusion:0.0:acc_deg_1} at $p=0$ to
\checknum{0.448}{fusion:0.1:acc_deg_1}--\checknum{0.726}{fusion:0.5:acc_deg_1}
for $p\ge 0.1$).
Terminal utility point estimates $U(1) = \text{acc}(S=1) - \text{acc}_{\text{null}}$
are negative for every $p$ (ranging from \checknum{-0.392}{fusion:0.1:U_1} at $p=0.1$
to \checknum{-0.133}{fusion:0.5:U_1} at $p=0.5$) and not monotone in $p$
(reported post hoc).

\paragraph{Why the normalized measures behave this way (post hoc).}
Reliance at clean input, $R(0)$, falls by more than an order of magnitude as $p$ grows because the model already makes similar predictions with and without view A. Because $\hat{R}$ and MI divide by $R(0)$, they are inflated when $R(0)$ is small, whereas raw reliance $R(1)$ decreases monotonically from \checknum{0.611}{fusion:0.0:R_1_raw} to \checknum{0.148}{fusion:0.5:R_1_raw}.
