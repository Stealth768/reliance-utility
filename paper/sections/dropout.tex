\section{Mitigation via Modality Dropout}
\label{sec:dropout}

To investigate whether reliance-utility misalignment can be mitigated during training, we evaluate a controlled case study applying modality dropout to a late fusion architecture. The architecture incorporates two MLP encoders and a learned quality-agnostic gate (evaluating representation $B$ without direct access to degradation severity $S$).

\subsection{Pre-Registered Hypotheses}
Prior to executing training experiments, we pre-registered three formal hypotheses in our experimental log:
\begin{description}
    \item[H1:] Models trained with higher modality-dropout probability $p \in \{0.0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5\}$ have lower $\mi$ under the \emph{removed} null (i.e., $\mi$ is monotonically non-increasing in $p$, allowing for ties within confidence intervals).
    \item[H2:] Clean accuracy drops by less than 3 percentage points between $p=0.0$ and $p=0.25$.
    \item[H3:] If H1 fails, the failure is reported directly without post hoc dataset alteration or grid tuning.
\end{description}

\subsection{Empirical Findings and Hypothesis Testing}
The results across the 20-run late-fusion grid on the Fashion-MNIST two-view benchmark ($p \in \{0.0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5\}$, 5 seeds each) are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:fusion_summary}.

\paragraph{Clean Accuracy Preservation (H2 Passed).}
Hypothesis H2 predicted that clean multimodal accuracy would drop by less than 3 percentage points between $p=0.0$ and $p=0.25$. As reported in Table~\ref{tab:fusion_summary}, clean accuracy at $p=0.0$ is $88.79\%$ and at $p=0.25$ is $88.43\%$, representing an empirical drop of merely $0.36$ percentage points ($95\%$ bootstrap CI well within the 3\% bound). Thus, \textbf{H2 is decisively supported}.

\paragraph{Non-Monotonic Reliance Misalignment (H1 Failed).}
Hypothesis H1 pre-registered that $\mi$ would be monotonically non-increasing in dropout probability $p$. Empirically, $\mi$ does not decrease with $p$; instead, it exhibits a non-monotonic pattern: $\mi = 1.66$ at $p=0.0$, rising sharply to $4.59$ at $p=0.1$ and $4.51$ at $p=0.25$, before slightly declining to $3.68$ at $p=0.50$. In accordance with protocol rule \textbf{H3}, we report this failure transparently without altering datasets or tuning the grid.

\paragraph{Mechanism Analysis.}
The root cause of the $\mi$ increase under modality dropout is a pronounced upward shift in unimodal baseline accuracy. When trained with $p=0.0$, the network under-utilizes the unimodal branch when modality A is ablated, attaining only $56.20\%$ null-removed accuracy. Because multimodal performance remains above $56\%$ across moderate severities, utility crosses zero late at $S^* = 0.50$. In contrast, training with modality dropout forces the network to master unimodal prediction ($83.96\%$ at $p=0.1$ and $84.77\%$ at $p=0.25$). Consequently, degraded inputs drop below the now-stringent unimodal benchmark much earlier, shifting $S^*$ to $0.25$. This dramatically expands the integration interval $[S^*, 1]$ over which the model still places non-zero behavioral reliance on modality A, yielding a higher aggregate misalignment index despite improved unimodal robustness.

Furthermore, as reported in Table~\ref{tab:fusion_summary}, baseline reliance $R(0)$ decreases from $0.33$ to $0.02$ as $p$ increases. Because normalized reliance is defined as $\hat{R}(S) = R(S)/R(0)$, this denominator contraction mechanically inflates $\hat{R}(1)$ ($1.85 \to 8.36$). In contrast, unnormalized terminal reliance $R(1)$ monotonically drops from $0.61$ to $0.15$, indicating that the inflated $\hat{R}(1)$ and elevated $\mi$ values under high dropout are largely driven by this normalization artifact.
