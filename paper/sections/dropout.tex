\section{Case study: modality dropout in a two-view proxy}
\label{sec:fusion}

\paragraph{Setup.}
To examine whether training with modality dropout changes reliance on a
degraded input, we train small late-fusion networks on Fashion-MNIST with
each image split into a left half (view A) and a right half (view B). This
is a \emph{two-view proxy} and not a multimodal benchmark. View A is
degraded by interpolation toward noise on a nine-point severity grid; the
no-input baseline replaces view A by a learned null embedding. The fusion
gate does not receive the severity. We train with view-A dropout
probabilities $p\in\{0,0.1,0.25,0.5\}$ and five seeds.

\paragraph{Pre-declared hypotheses.}
We declared before training: (H1) the Misalignment Index is non-increasing
in $p$; (H2) clean accuracy falls by less than three points between $p=0$
and $p=0.25$.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:fusion} reports the outcome. H2 holds. H1 does \emph{not}:
the Misalignment Index is higher for every $p>0$ than for $p=0$. Between
$p=0$ and $p=0.25$, clean accuracy changes from
\checknum{0.8879}{fusion:0.0:clean_acc} to
\checknum{0.8843}{fusion:0.25:clean_acc}, a decrease of only
\checknum{0.36}{fusion:diff_0_25:clean_acc_pp} percentage points,
well within the pre-declared three-point bound.
Dropout-trained models achieve much higher accuracy when view A is removed
(increasing from \checknum{0.5620}{fusion:0.0:acc_null_removed} at $p=0$ to
\checknum{0.8396}{fusion:0.1:acc_null_removed}--\checknum{0.8584}{fusion:0.5:acc_null_removed}
for $p\ge 0.1$) and when it is fully degraded
(increasing from \checknum{0.2761}{fusion:0.0:acc_deg_1} at $p=0$ to
\checknum{0.4480}{fusion:0.1:acc_deg_1}--\checknum{0.7256}{fusion:0.5:acc_deg_1}
for $p\ge 0.1$).
Terminal utility point estimates $U(1) = \text{acc}(S=1) - \text{acc}_{\text{null}}$
are negative for every $p$ (ranging from \checknum{-0.3916}{fusion:0.1:U_1} at $p=0.1$
to \checknum{-0.1327}{fusion:0.5:U_1} at $p=0.5$; hierarchical 95\% bootstrap
intervals reported in Table~\ref{tab:fusion}) and not monotone in $p$
(reported post hoc).

\paragraph{Why the normalized measures behave this way (post hoc).}
Reliance at clean input, $R(0)$, falls by more than an order of
magnitude as $p$ grows, because a model trained with dropout already
makes similar predictions with and without view A. Since $\hat R$ and the
Misalignment Index divide by $R(0)$, they are inflated when $R(0)$ is
small, and their comparison across $p$ is not informative. Raw terminal
reliance $R(1)$ decreases with $p$ (falling monotonically from
\checknum{0.6106}{fusion:0.0:R_1_raw} at $p=0$ to
\checknum{0.4516}{fusion:0.1:R_1_raw} at $p=0.1$,
\checknum{0.3303}{fusion:0.25:R_1_raw} at $p=0.25$, and
\checknum{0.1478}{fusion:0.5:R_1_raw} at $p=0.5$). We
report this as a post hoc observation and not as support for H1.

\paragraph{Scope.}
This is a small proxy with one dataset and simple encoders. It shows that
dropout preserves clean accuracy and improves accuracy on degraded input
while lowering raw reliance on it, and it exposes a weakness of
$R(0)$-normalized measures. It does not show a general property of large
multimodal models.
