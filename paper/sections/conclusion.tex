\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Multimodal artificial intelligence models frequently maintain reliance on input modalities even when those modalities have ceased to provide task-relevant information. In this work, we introduced a formal, black-box diagnostic framework that decomposes degradation behavior into task utility and distributional reliance along calibrated severity paths. Through controlled synthetic experiments with oracle benchmarks and empirical evaluations across three open-weight vision-language models, we showed that semantically structured corruptions (swapped images) systematically sustain higher terminal reliance than unstructured noise, producing utility inversions where relying on the degraded input harms accuracy relative to ignoring it entirely. Furthermore, our baseline sensitivity analysis revealed that token-structure artifacts can substantially confound visual dependence measurements unless multi-baseline evaluations are conducted. By providing an open, reproducible framework and establishing rigorous confidence intervals for reliance-utility alignment, this work offers intelligent systems researchers and practitioners a principled diagnostic tool to evaluate multimodal robustness and prevent deceptive modality dependence in high-stakes applications.
